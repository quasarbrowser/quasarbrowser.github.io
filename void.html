Overview

What is Void Mode?

Void Mode is Quasar's on/off switch for anonymous routing. When it is active, nothing travels to a website directly — every request is wrapped, handed to four separate relays, unwrapped only at the very end, and thrown away afterwards.

Purpose
Hide the person, keep the content. The website still works — images, video, forms, downloads — but it never learns who asked.
Layers
Four encrypted hops (Lobby → corepoint → Escape → Void4) with a per-hop key, so no single relay holds the full path.
Identity
An ephemeral alias replaces your IP at hop one. It is random, single-use, and dies with the request.
Fingerprint
Randomised per session. Canvas, WebGL, audio, fonts and screen metrics are masked before any page script runs.
Tracking
Stripped in transit. Ad, tracker and fingerprint requests are dropped by the built-in engine, not by an extension.
Storage
Nothing to leak. No telemetry, no usage stats, no crash reports leaving the device.
Uploads
Always confirmed. File uploads, forms and clipboard reads ask you first, even inside a void session.
Plain English: think of Void Mode as a chain of four locked rooms. Each room can open the parcel it just received and put it in a new locked box for the next room — but no room can look inside the box it is about to hand over. That is why no single relay can sell your identity or your destination.

The alias, the keys and the relay state are destroyed when the request completes. There is no log to subpoena and no history to correlate — the packet simply stops existing.

How It Works

The four sealed hops

Each hop only ever holds one key. A relay can open the layer addressed to it and re-seal for the next hop — it can never read the whole route. That separation is the entire trick.

HOP 01

Lobby

Identity Masked

The first door. Lobby terminates your connection, discards your real IP, and mints a random alias for this one request. Your connection is then re-encrypted with the hop-1 key and pushed to the next relay. Lobby knows a request exists; it does not know where it came from and cannot read where it is going.

× Your real IP
× Your destination
× Your page content
✓ Mints the alias

HOP 02

corepoint

Sealed Relay

The encryption layer of the chain. corepoint opens layer 1, performs the integrity check, and re-seals the payload with the hop-2 key before handing it to Escape. Because the alias replaced your address at Lobby, corepoint has nothing to log — it literally cannot see the origin.

× The original source
× The final destination
× Layer 2 onward
✓ Opens layer 1
✓ Verifies integrity
✓ Wraps layer 2
HOP 03

Escape

Tamper-Proof

The separation layer. Escape carries a bundle it is deliberately unable to open, running a tamper check on the wrapper only. If a single bit was altered in transit, the packet dies here instead of reaching the website. It then re-seals with the hop-3 key for the final hop.

× The payload it carries
× Who is behind the alias
× Anything after Void4
✓ Tamper verification
✓ Replay rejection
✓ Wraps layer 3
HOP 04

Void4

Final Exit

The only relay that touches the real website. Void4 verifies the signature chain, unwraps the last layer and makes the outbound request from its own IP. The website therefore sees Void4 — and Void4 sees only the address you asked for, never the person who asked.

× Your identity
× Your alias history
× Anything after the website

Inside

What is inside the void

The pipeline is the transport. These are the modules that decide what gets sent at all, what gets stripped, and what has to ask you first. All of them are built in — no extension store, no configuration required.

Ad & Tracker Blocker

Every request is inspected before it leaves. Banners, pop-ups, tracking pixels, third-party cookies and malvertising hosts are dropped at the network layer, so the script never even executes on your machine.

Network layer Zero extensions

Fingerprint Defence

Canvas, WebGL, audio context, font list, screen and hardware metrics are randomised per session. You are presented as one of millions of identical generic devices instead of one unique, sellable profile.

Per-session Anti-correlation

Sealed Pipes

Every hop uses authenticated encryption with a key derived separately for that hop. Replay is rejected, tampering is detected, and a compromised relay still cannot read the rest of the route.

AEAD Per-hop keys

Ghost Networking

Traffic leaves through peer relays rather than a central provider. The exit IP belongs to Void4, and the chain in between can be reshuffled per session so no single observer can build a map of your browsing.

Peer relays Rotating chain

Upload Guard

Sending a file, submitting a form, granting clipboard or camera access — each one raises a permission prompt first. Void Mode never turns "I was browsing privately" into "everything I did was hidden".

Per-action prompt Never silent

Zero Telemetry

No phone-home, no usage analytics, no crash reports to a remote server, no A/B buckets, no "anonymous" identifier. If Quasar cannot see it, it cannot send it.

Privacy

What the relays cannot learn

Privacy here is structural, not a promise. The design makes certain facts physically unavailable at each stage — so there is nothing for a relay, an operator or a subpoena to obtain.

For the relays

  • • No relay holds the full key chain, so none can decrypt end to end.
  • • Aliases are random, single-use and destroyed after the request.
  • • A relay that tampers with a packet kills it at the next hop.
  • • A replayed packet is rejected by the integrity counter.

For websites

  • • They receive Void4's IP, not yours.
  • • Your device profile is generic and randomised per session.
  • • Tracker, pixel and fingerprint scripts are dropped before they run.
  • • Session cookies die with the void session.

For everyone else

  • • Your ISP sees an encrypted relay connection, nothing about destinations.
  • • Your network operator sees a rotating chain of relays.
  • • Quasar itself keeps no server-side record to hand over.
  • • There is no account, no profile and no identifier to leak.
Honest limits. Void Mode protects the transport, not what you type. Signing into an account, uploading a document or filling a form still tells that site who you are — that is why Upload Guard asks every time. And a void session cannot hide traffic that your own device generates outside the browser.
Developers

Publish your site to Void Search

Void Search is Quasar's built-in discovery engine. To appear in it you ship one small plain-text file called index.void next to your index.html. No SDK, no build step, no tracking.

1 · Folder layout

your-site/
  → index.html (your page)
  → index.void (Void Search listing)
  → void.png (preview image)
  → banner.png (optional, 468×60)
The filename must be exactly index.void and it must sit in the same folder as index.html. Image paths are relative to that folder. One file per site — sub-pages are discovered through the same listing.
index.void — Quasar Browser Website
<title>Quasar Browser - Your Browser, Your Rules</title> <void name="banner" content="void.png"> <void name="description" content="A privacy-focused browser that blocks third-party tracking, resists fingerprinting, and routes every request through four encrypted hops."> <void name="keywords" content="Quasar Browser, privacy browser, ad blocker, tracker blocker, anti-fingerprinting, Void Mode, encrypted browsing"> <void name="author" content="Quasar Browser"> <void property="void:type" content="website"> <void property="void:title" content="Quasar Browser - Private Browsing with Void Mode"> <void property="void:description" content="Block trackers, defeat fingerprinting and browse through four sealed hops. No telemetry, no data collected."> <void property="void:image" content="void.png"> <void protocole="Quasar Browser" content="QB">

2 · Tag reference

Tag Attributes Example value Status Purpose
<title> text content Quasar Browser Required Site name shown as the heading of your Void Search result.
<void name="banner"> name, content void.png Optional Top banner image. Recommended size 468×60 px so it does not get cropped.
<void name="description"> name, content Short paragraph, roughly 120–160 characters Required The full description body of your listing.
<void name="keywords"> name, content privacy browser, ad blocker, ... Required Comma-separated terms Void Search matches against. 4–8 strong terms work best.
<void name="author"> name, content Quasar Browser Optional Publisher credit shown under the listing.
<void property="void:type"> property, content website Required Declares the listing type to the search engine.
<void property="void:title"> property, content Quasar Browser - Private Browsing Required Headline used in results and previews.
<void property="void:description"> property, content One-line summary Required The short snippet shown under the headline in results.
<void property="void:image"> property, content void.png Required Thumbnail image for the result card. Square images render best.
<void protocole="Quasar Browser"> protocole, content QB Required Marks the site as a native Quasar Browser listing. Note the spelling: protocole, not protocol.

3 · Pre-publish checklist

  • ✓File is named exactly index.void and sits beside index.html.
  • ✓All six required tags are present and closed.
  • ✓Image paths exist and match the real filenames, including the extension.
  • ✓Banner, if used, is 468×60 px and under ~60 KB.
  • ✓keywords uses comma separators and describes your site, not your brand slogan.
  • ✓No # comment markers carried over from the template are left in production.
  • ✓The final line is protocole="Quasar Browser" with content QB.
